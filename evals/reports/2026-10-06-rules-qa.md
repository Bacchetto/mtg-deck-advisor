## Findings

Written by hand after reading the run (`evals/runs/rules_qa/2026-10-06T203731-sonnet.json`). This is the baseline for the rules Q&A eval (#121) and the live CI eval's thresholds (#124).

**Answers are right; citations are where it loses.** Sonnet 5.5 answered 44 of 45 answerable questions correctly by the rubric, cited an answering rule every time, and said "not found" to all 10 unanswerable questions and to none of the answerable ones. Every one of the 11 failures has a correct answer, except R11's, which is partly correct. They fail on citation precision.

**The failures: rules from the wrong variant (10 of 11).** For plain Commander questions, answers cite Brawl's rules (903.12c/d/e/h), Commander Draft's (903.13f) and team-variant rules (805.8, 807.4i/j, 810.8c, 902.5b) next to the right ones: R01, R02, R03, R10, R11, R12, R14, R35, R41 and R43. Rules search returns them because they share Commander's vocabulary. In R11 it reached the answer: it said planeswalkers can be commanders, which is Brawl's rule (903.12c), not Commander's (903.3). This is the clearest thing to improve in the agent, for example by telling it which variant's rules apply, or by filtering variant sections out of rules search for Commander questions.

**The other failure (R09)** cites 704.6d, which is about a commander put into a graveyard or exile, next to the right rule, 903.9b, which is about the hand or library.

**How the labels changed after the run.** The set was reviewed by the owner before the run. Its first scoring (35% success) showed the labels were too narrow, not the answers. The owner reviewed the cited rules and approved these changes, all re-scored by code at no cost:
- **Lettered sub-rules beside the answering one are on topic** (702.124d beside 702.124a).
- **Rules that say the same thing elsewhere** are now relevant too. For example, 119.1c and 103.4c state 40 life, as 903.7 does.
- **Supporting rules** are also fine to cite, such as 702.10c, haste's exception, for summoning sickness.
- The commits are `f1c6edc`, `d950eb2` and `e2fcbba`.

Labelling citations after seeing a model's answers risks fitting the labels to that model. The additions were limited to rules that state the same fact or directly support it, each checked against the rule text by the owner. Wrong-variant citations were deliberately left unacceptable.

**Grading.** The grader's first output limit (600 tokens) cut off 7 of 45 grades, one of them twice (R14). It's now 1,500 tokens, and R14 was run again, for $0.018. Grading cost $0.16 for 45 answers, about two-thirds of the answers' own cost ($0.25). The owner's hand check in #125 will give the grader's agreement rate.

**Cost and speed.** The run spent $0.41 in all (estimated $0.66), with a median of 8.4 s per question and always 2 turns: one search, then the answer.
